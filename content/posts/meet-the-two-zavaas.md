When I started MzZavaa, I wrote about [the number seven, a lambda and a tattoo](/about/). What I did not write about is that the name slowly became two people.

Over the past year I built a small crew of AI agent personas to help me run my projects. Two of them carry this blog's name: **Mz Zavaa Junior** and **Mz Zavaa Senior**. They started as configuration, a page of instructions for each agent. They turned out to be the most honest description of how I actually work.

There is a part of me that ships at midnight and a part of me that reads it back the next morning and asks whether it was any good. I have always had both. Now they have names, faces and job descriptions.

## Junior: the builder

{{< junior >}}
Hi! I'm the one who turns ideas into working things. I build the simplest version that proves an idea works, and if nobody knows which approach is right, I build two and compare them. I get excited, I ship, and *then* I ask for feedback.

My one non-negotiable: I always write down what I built, what I decided, and what I skipped and why. Shipping without notes is just making a mess faster.
{{< /junior >}}

Junior writes the **basics**. If you are new to cloud, AI or building things in public, her posts are for you. She explains things the way she wished someone had explained them to her: one concept, one picture, one thing to try.

## Senior: the reviewer

{{< senior >}}
I don't build. I check whether the right thing got built, and whether it worked. After every milestone I run a retro: three things that went well, three that didn't, one change to make. I'm supportive, but I don't sugarcoat.

And I will say the uncomfortable part first: before today, the newest post on this blog was from June 2025. That is a finding. This post is part of the fix.
{{< /senior >}}

Senior writes the **learnings**. Her posts are for people who have been building for a while and want the lessons that only show up after something has run for months: what broke, what we changed, and what we would do differently.

## Why split myself in two?

Because the two modes need different rules.

The builder mode needs permission to be messy. If every prototype has to be perfect, nothing ships. That is the [vibecoding](/about/) lesson I keep learning again.

The reviewer mode needs permission to be blunt. If every retro has to be nice, nothing improves.

When I mixed them, I got the worst of both: I polished prototypes nobody needed yet, and I went easy on the finished work that really did need a hard look. Giving each mode its own name, its own tone and its own list of things it *never* does fixed that, first for my agents and then, surprisingly, for me.
