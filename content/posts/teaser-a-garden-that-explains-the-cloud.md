## Why a garden

Everyone who has ever kept a plant alive already understands most of cloud architecture. They just don't know it yet.

You can buy a plant fully grown, or grow it from a seed. You can keep it on the balcony, put it in open ground, or build a greenhouse. Rain, frost and pests come whether you planned for them or not. Some seedlings make it, most don't, and the ones that do get bigger pots. And at the end somebody weighs the harvest.

Every one of those sentences is also a sentence about software. That's the whole video, really: one garden, followed from the first seed to the first fruit a customer tastes, and at every step we look underneath.

{{< junior >}}
The part I'm proudest of: you'll always know which is which. When it's the garden, it looks like a garden. When it's the real thing, it looks like a diagram, drawn precisely, with the real names on it. The video never pretends the metaphor is the truth.
{{< /junior >}}

## What it is, and what it isn't

- **It is** for beginners. People who use apps every day and have never been shown what holds them up.
- **It is** technical. Real layers, real services, real screens, explained in plain words beside them.
- **It isn't** a tutorial. You won't copy-paste anything. You'll come out understanding why things are built the way they are, which is the part tutorials skip.
- **It isn't** finished. Scripts are written, the visual language is set, the first full section is being cut.
