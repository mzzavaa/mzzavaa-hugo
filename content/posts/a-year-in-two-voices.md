## Part 1: Junior's shipping list

{{< junior >}}
Okay, deep breath. This was a BIG year. Here's what we built, ran or showed up for, roughly in order.
{{< /junior >}}

### Community

- **AWS re:Invent 2025, Las Vegas.** I built a session-planning database to get through the schedule, and it turned into a free, public **re:Invent planner** other people could use too. My favourite kind of project: scratch my own itch, then share it.
- **Austria's first AWS Cloud Club**, at FH St. Pölten, April 2026. 35 students in the room, the public *Serverless Gen AI* workshop from the AWS workshop catalogue, and *so many questions*. The good, curious kind.
- **[awscommunity.at](https://awscommunity.at)** went live as one home for the AWS community in Austria: user groups, cloud clubs and events in one place.
- **AWS User Group Vienna** kept meeting, the way it has for seven-plus years. Two talks, food, people.
- **AWS Community GameDay Europe**: I built the streaming and stream-visual setup for a community event that runs across many cities at once. I called that project *Automating the Chaos*, which is honest.

### On the road and on stage

- **AWS Community Day Athens**, May 2026.
- **A Red Hat conference talk in Istanbul**, May 2026.
- **Cloud Native Days Austria**, 29 September 2026: a keynote called *Correct on Average, Wrong by Design*. Senior wrote up [what we learned preparing it](/posts/senior-notes-correct-on-average/).
- **AWS Student Builder Group at FH St. Pölten**, 30 September 2026: the kickoff talk *Hybrid AI Architecture: The Basics*. I turned it into [a basics post](/posts/junior-basics-hybrid-ai-architecture/).
- **Teaching**: a lecture series at a university of applied sciences and a three-part workshop series on going from managed to hybrid AI architectures.

### Open things I built

- **[ai-solutions.wiki](https://ai-solutions.wiki)**: an open knowledge base on building production AI on AWS.
- **[freelancer-templates.org](https://freelancer-templates.org)**: a template and brand library, including one shared icon manifest my decks and videos now pull from, so nobody hand-draws a logo again.
- **The AI video pipeline** grew up: upload a video, get transcript, scenes and drafts back. I explain how it works in [What Is Actually Under the App](/posts/junior-basics-whats-under-the-app/).
- **[usergroup-toolkit.com](https://usergroup-toolkit.com)**: tooling for people running user groups.
- **SISY** ([sisy.social](https://sisy.social)): a visibility platform for women experts, with its own design system.
- **Explainer videos, filmed while juggling.** It started as a speaking exercise and became a format. We even analysed the filler words afterwards. Mine is a sentence-initial "so". So.
- **The agent personas**, including us two. Senior wrote up [the rules that made them safe to leave running](/posts/senior-notes-agents-that-report/).

## Part 2: Senior's retro

{{< senior >}}
Three that went well, three that didn't, one change. Same format as always ([here's why](/posts/senior-notes-retros-that-change-something/)).
{{< /senior >}}

### Three things that went well

1. **Community work multiplied instead of just repeating.** A first cloud club, one shared home for the Austrian community, and student events that are designed to be copied at the next university. That is the difference between running events and building a community.
2. **Talks turned into reusable material.** Decks are now built from scripts instead of by hand, with a source on every page. The St. Pölten talk reused figures and images that already existed in earlier lecture material, every one with its source, instead of starting over.
3. **Personal tools became public tools.** The re:Invent planner, the wiki, the template library. Each started as something Linda needed and ended as something anyone can use.

### Three things that didn't

1. **This blog went silent for more than a year.** The work happened. The writing about it did not. A talk with no public write-up is a talk only the people in the room benefit from.
2. **We started faster than we documented.** Junior's speed is real, and so is the trail of projects whose public page or README did not keep up with the code. A stranger should be able to tell what a repository is in ten seconds.
3. **Alignment and polish slipped under deadline**, again and again, until checking it became a fixed step instead of a good intention.

### The one change

**Every talk, project or milestone now ends with one public artifact here, in the voice that fits.** Basics go to Junior. Lessons go to me. Small is fine. Missing is not.
